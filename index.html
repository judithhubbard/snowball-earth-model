Model equations

Ninety latitude bands, 2° each. For band $i$ with $x_i = \sin\varphi_i$ at its midpoint, land fraction $f_i$, and temperature $T_i$:

\[ C_i \frac{dT_i}{dt} = Q\,s(x_i)\,(1-\alpha_i) \;-\; \big(A - \Delta F + B\,T_i\big) \;-\; C_{\rm tr}\,(T_i - \bar T) \]

Sunlight by latitude: $s(x) = 1 - 0.241\,(3x^2 - 1)$, with $Q = 340$ W m⁻² × (solar constant / 100%).

Albedo from surface type and ice cover, where $\ell_i$ and $o_i$ are the ice-covered fractions of the land and ocean in the band (0 above the threshold + 3 °C, 1 below the threshold − 3 °C, linear between; thresholds −1 °C for sea ice, −8 °C for land ice):

\[ \alpha_i = f_i\big[(1-\ell_i)\,0.35 + \ell_i\,0.65\big] + (1-f_i)\big[(1-o_i)\,0.28 + o_i\,0.65\big] \]

Thermal emission $A + B T$ with $A = 196$ W m⁻², $B = 2.1$ W m⁻² °C⁻¹ at 420 ppm CO₂. Extra greenhouse forcing from CO₂ at concentration $c$:

\[ \Delta F = 5.35\,\ln\frac{c}{420} + 0.39\left[\ln\frac{c}{420}\right]^2 \quad\text{(second term only for } c > 420\text{)} \]

Heat transport toward the global mean with $C_{\rm tr} = 2.7$ W m⁻² °C⁻¹. Heat capacity $C_i = 0.5 f_i + 8\,(1-f_i)$ W yr m⁻² °C⁻¹ (ocean responds slowly, land quickly).

Carbon cycle, when on. Volcanic input $V = 0.044$ ppm yr⁻¹ × the volcanic multiplier. Weathering on ice-free land, faster where warm and at higher CO₂:

\[ \frac{dc}{dt} = V - W, \qquad W = k\,(1 + 7b)\,V_0 \left(\frac{c}{420}\right)^{0.3} \sum_i w_i\, f_i\,(1-\ell_i)\, e^{(T_i - 15)/13.7} \]

where $w_i$ is the band's fraction of Earth's surface, $b$ is the fraction of land that is fresh basalt (weathering 8× average crust), and $k$ is fixed so that $W = V$ for the modern Earth at the pre-industrial 280 ppm.

Half of the volcanic CO₂ is released under the sea. The ocean holds dissolved CO₂ in an amount $O$ (in atmospheric-ppm equivalent) that, over open water, equilibrates with the air in ~1000 years toward $O = 0.5\,c$. Under sea ice, exchange is cut to the open-water fraction plus 10% of the ice-covered area (cracks and thin ice), so submarine CO₂ accumulates in the ocean and is released when the ice breaks up:

\[ \frac{dc}{dt} = 0.5\,V - W + E, \qquad \frac{dO}{dt} = 0.5\,V - E, \qquad E = \frac{O - 0.5\,c}{1.5}\,\frac{\text{contact}}{1000\ \text{yr}} \]

What the Neoproterozoic scenario assumes, and why

  • Sun 94%: its luminosity at 700 Ma from standard solar evolution.
  • Low-latitude supercontinent: Rodinia straddled the equator.
  • Fresh basalt 7% of land: the Franklin large igneous province erupted across equatorial Laurentia at 717 Ma, the age of the Sturtian onset (~1.5% of land), with several other Cryogenian provinces; 7% is the size of the largest known province (CAMP). Basalt consumes CO₂ ~8× faster than average crust per unit area (Dessert et al. 2003).
  • Volcanism 0.5×: Neoproterozoic outgassing is not measured. Continental-arc activity, traced by detrital zircons, was at a minimum through the Cryogenian (McKenzie et al. 2016, Science), which argues for reduced CO₂ output; half of modern is within the range considered.

With these values CO₂ falls below this model's freezing point of ~150 ppm (with a 94% Sun). Comprehensive climate models freeze at ~100–300 ppm under the same Sun. Ice thresholds are set from present-day observations: sea-ice edges sit at annual mean air temperatures of about −1 to −5 °C, ice-sheet margins near −8 °C; the model uses −1 °C for sea ice, at the warm end of that range, and consequently has somewhat more sea ice than today (edge at ~58° rather than ~62°). One planetary albedo, 0.65, is used for all ice. The exit from the frozen state occurs at ~0.25 bar of CO₂; published estimates range from 0.1 to over 0.3 bar depending on ice albedo and dust. Under full ice cover the model's weathering is zero: there is no exposed rock and, on a frozen planet, almost no rain. Half of volcanic CO₂ enters the ocean rather than the air. With 10% of the ice-covered ocean still exchanging gas through cracks and thin ice, the ocean stays close to equilibrium with the air and simply holds a third of the added carbon, which makes the frozen interval about 1.5× longer than if all volcanic CO₂ went to the air (~16 Myr in the scenario). A perfectly sealed ocean would store the submarine half entirely, doubling the interval and releasing the stored CO₂ when the ice breaks; how leaky the ice was is not known.
